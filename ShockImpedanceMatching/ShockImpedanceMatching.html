Theory & Governing Equations ▼

Rankine–Hugoniot jump conditions (1D planar shock, steady wave):

Conservation of mass ρ0 Us = ρ (Us − Up)
Conservation of momentum P − P0 = ρ0 Us Up

Linear / quadratic Us–Up material model (empirical fit for many solids):

Shock velocity – particle velocity relation Us = C0 + s Up + s2 Up2

Substituting into the momentum equation gives pressure as a function of particle velocity (set s2 = 0 for linear model):

Hugoniot P–Up curve P(Up) = ρ0 (C0 + s Up + s2 Up2) Up

Incident shock interaction — a steady shock wave in Material A (pressure P1) arrives at the interface with Material B. The interaction sends a reflected wave back into A and a transmitted shock into B:

Incident shock state on A’s principal Hugoniot P1 = ρ0,A (C0,A + sA Up1 + s2,A Up12) Up1
Reflected Hugoniot (mirror method) Pr(Up) = ρ0,A (C0,A + sA Up,m + s2,A Up,m2) Up,m
Up,m = 2 Up1 − Up

Two-shock interaction — when a second shock wave in Material B (pressure P1B) arrives from the opposite side simultaneously with A’s shock, the interface state is found by balancing both reflected Hugoniots:

Material A reflected Hugoniot (center at Up1,A) PA(Up) = ρ0,A (C0,A + sA (2Up1,A − Up) + s2,A (2Up1,A − Up)2) (2Up1,A − Up)
Material B reflected Hugoniot (center at −Up1,B) PB(Up) = ρ0,B (C0,B + sB (2Up1,B + Up) + s2,B (2Up1,B + Up)2) (2Up1,B + Up)

The interface solution satisfies PA(Up) = PB(Up). Setting P1B = 0 recovers the single-shock case exactly.

Impedance matching — at the flyer/target interface, pressure and particle velocity are continuous:

Target Hugoniot (centered at rest) Pt(Up) = ρ0,t (C0,t + st Up + s2,t Up2) Up
Flyer reflected Hugoniot (centered at Vimp) Pf(Up) = ρ0,f (C0,f + sf ΔUp + s2,f ΔUp2) ΔUp
ΔUp = Vimp − Up

Detonation impedance matching — a steady detonation wave propagates through the explosive at velocity D. The Chapman–Jouguet (CJ) state is the sonic plane at the end of the reaction zone. The explosive products are modeled as a γ-law gas:

Polytropic exponent γ = ρ0 D2 ⁄ PCJ − 1
CJ particle velocity Up,CJ = D ⁄ (γ + 1)
CJ sound speed cCJ = γ D ⁄ (γ + 1)
CJ density ρCJ = ρ0 (γ + 1) ⁄ γ

The release of the products from the CJ state follows the γ-law isentrope. Along the backward C+ characteristic, the Riemann invariant is conserved:

Riemann invariant up + 2c ⁄ (γ − 1) = Up,CJ + 2cCJ ⁄ (γ − 1)
Product isentrope (P ≤ PCJ) Up(P) = Up,CJ + (2cCJ ⁄ (γ − 1)) (1 − (P ⁄ PCJ)(γ − 1) ⁄ 2γ)

For an overdriven detonation (P > PCJ), the reflected shock Hugoniot in the products is used:

Product reflected shock (P > PCJ) Up(P) = Up,CJ − (P − PCJ) √ 2 ⁄ ρCJ ((γ+1)P + (γ−1)PCJ) 

In all three configurations, the interface particle velocity Up is found where pressure is continuous across the interface: PA(Up) = PB(Up). The function PA(Up) is the flyer reflected Hugoniot, the mirror-method reflected Hugoniot, or the γ-law product isentrope depending on the configuration. The solution is obtained via numerical root finding (bisection method) in each case.

Units note: With ρ0 in g/cm³, velocities in km/s, the product ρ0 Us Up yields pressure directly in GPa.

Assumptions & Limitations ▼
  • The materials listed under Elements, Alloys, Synthetics, Compounds, and Explosives are taken from Paul Cooper's textbook Explosives Engineering (see references).
  • One-dimensional planar geometry (no edge release, no 2D/3D effects)
  • Linear / quadratic Us–Up relationship approximates the shock state (empirical fit)
  • Both materials are initially at zero pressure and ambient temperature
  • Release is approximated by the reflected Hugoniot (isentropic approximation)
  • No phase transitions, chemical reactions, or strength effects
  • Elastic precursor neglected (hydrostatic approximation)
  • No spallation or fracture
  • Steady, planar shock waves (no wave profile evolution)
